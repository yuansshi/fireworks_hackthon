**Slide 1 (0–8 s).** This is PhishGuard: an open NVIDIA Nemotron model, fine-tuned on Fireworks, that beats a frontier model at screening scams.

**Slide 2 (8–23 s).** Fraud teams must screen every email, text, link and call before customers act. Frontier models are accurate, but at about sixteen hundred dollars per million messages, they are too expensive to run on everything.

**Slide 4 (33–42 s).** Every system answered the same cases with the same prompt: Kimi K3 and DeepSeek as frontier baselines, Nemotron untuned, and our fine-tune.

**Slide 5 (42–50 s).** Training was light: a rank sixteen LoRA, one epoch, about eleven minutes and two dollars on Fireworks Serverless Training.

**Slide 6 (50–65 s).** The result: ninety-seven point eight percent accuracy, versus ninety-six point two for Kimi K3. That is forty-three percent fewer errors, significant at p equals point zero four, and about seven times cheaper.

**Slide 7 (65–73 s).** On held-out examples, our recorded verdicts match live Kimi K3 calls at a fraction of the cost. Both miss the tricky URL.

**Slide 8 (73–93 s).** After hearing a quarter of a call, we flag ninety-six percent of scams with two percent false alarms; Kimi raises fourteen percent. The tradeoff: on a later, unseen email corpus and on new synthetic scams, the frontier generalizes better, so we would refresh the adapter with new data.
